Frequently asked questions

I filed my extension in April. Am I still getting hit with penalties? +

Possibly. An extension extends your filing deadline, not your payment deadline. If you owed taxes on April 15 and didn't pay the full amount, the IRS is charging failure-to-pay penalties (0.5% per month) plus interest until you settle up. The good news: filing now stops the failure-to-pay clock faster than waiting until October. The longer you sit on it, the bigger the bill.

How fast can you actually get my return filed? +

Most extension returns take 3–5 weeks from intake call to e-file. The variable isn't us — it's how quickly documents come together. Start now and you're done by Labor Day. Start in mid-September and you're in line with everyone else trying to beat October 15. That math doesn't get better the longer you wait.

My return is "pretty simple." Do I really need a CPA? +

If it were truly simple, you wouldn't have extended. Something about this return felt big enough to push past April. That something usually rewards an expert: equity comp, K-1s, multi-state, rental, business income, a big year-over-year change. On the call we tell you straight up whether it's worth hiring us or whether you can finish it yourself.

Yes — this is the work we do all day. RSUs, ISOs, NSOs, ESPPs, AMT, founder shares, multi-state allocation, S-corp K-1s, partnership returns, rental income, foreign income. The harder it is, the more value we add. The 30-minute call covers exactly what's in your return so there are no surprises.
