The Real Cost

Six RSU mistakes that quietly cost tech workers a fortune.

Standard tax prep treats your RSU income like any other W-2 line. That's how most people end up owing five figures in April with no warning. Here's what we look for.

01

Trusting the default 22% withholding

Most employers withhold the IRS supplemental rate of 22% on RSU vests. If you're in the 32% or 37% bracket, you're already underpaid — and the bill lands in April with interest and penalty risk.

02

Selling on a calendar, not a plan

"Sell-to-cover, sell the rest immediately" feels safe. Sometimes it is. Sometimes you're walking past long-term capital gains rates, a low-income year window, or a charitable strategy worth tens of thousands.

03

Wrong state allocation after a move

RSU income is allocated based on where you lived during the vesting period — not where you live now. Moved from CA to TX? Half your vest might still owe California. Most software gets this silently wrong.

04

Missing quarterly estimates

A six-figure RSU vest in Q2 without an estimated payment is a guaranteed underpayment penalty by year-end. We project your tax liability the moment shares vest and pay in real time.

05

Stacking ISO exercises into AMT

If you also hold ISOs, your RSU income is precisely what pushes you into Alternative Minimum Tax. The fix is timing — but only if someone is actually modeling it before you exercise.

06

Treating ESPP like a free lunch

ESPP discount vs. capital gain timing rules confuse most filers and most CPAs. Qualifying vs. disqualifying dispositions can swing your tax bill by thousands per cycle.

Frequently asked questions

My company already withholds taxes on my RSU vests. Do I really need a CPA? +

Probably yes. Most employers withhold the IRS default supplemental rate of 22% on RSU income. If your total income puts you in the 32% or 37% federal bracket — which is the case for most senior tech ICs and managers — you're underpaid by 10–15% of your vest value. A CPA models your full income and tells you exactly what additional estimated payment to make, when to make it, and how to avoid the underpayment penalty.

I just got my first RSU vest. Is it too early to plan? +

It's actually the best time. The decisions you make in your first vesting year — sell vs. hold, quarterly estimates, state allocation, ESPP enrollment — set the pattern for the next four years. Getting it right now saves significantly more than fixing it later, and there's no minimum income to work with us.

I sold my RSUs the day they vested. Did I mess up? +

Sell-at-vest is often the right default — it locks in the after-tax value and removes single-stock concentration risk. But it's not always optimal. If you held for a year, you could've gotten long-term capital gains rates on the appreciation. If you sold into a high-income year, you may have stacked at the wrong time. We model the alternatives so you stop guessing and start choosing.

What's the difference between RSU vesting and exercising stock options? +

RSUs are taxed automatically the moment they vest. There's no decision — the IRS counts the fair market value of vested shares as W-2 income. Stock options (ISOs and NSOs) have an exercise event you control, which means you can choose timing. The two are taxed very differently and need different strategies. If you have both, your plan has to coordinate them.
